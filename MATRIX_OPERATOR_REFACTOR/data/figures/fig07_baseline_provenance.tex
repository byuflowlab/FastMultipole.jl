\fmnote{\textbf{Machines / regimes:} (a) \texttt{m13h-1-1} Xeon Platinum 8568Y+ with OpenBLAS pinned to 1
thread, and \texttt{m12-2-5} EPYC 7763 with 72 BLAS threads; both BLAS installs tuned. (b) \texttt{m13h-1-1}
NVIDIA H200, hand-written fused scalar CUDA kernel --- deliberately \emph{not} cuBLAS. (c)
\texttt{tmpfac-126-17}, Apple M2 laptop, Julia 1.12.5, BLAS=1, batch 4096. All Float64.
\textbf{Data:} \texttt{impl\_performance\_baseline/\{m13h-1-1/dense\_vs\_loop\_blas1.csv,
m12-2-5/dense\_vs\_loop\_blas72.csv, m13h-1-1/dense\_gpu.csv\}},
\texttt{axis\_swap/tmpfac-126-17.et.byu.edu/m2l\_variants\_blas1.csv}
(tables \texttt{fig07a\_*}, \texttt{fig07b\_*}, \texttt{fig07c\_*}).
\textbf{Reads as:} this is the honest arc of the project. Task 008c projected 31--118$\times$ for a dense
axis swap over the recurrence, with multi-thread BLAS \emph{losing} below batch $\approx8$ (a). It also showed
that per-expansion host--device transfer floors out worse than the best CPU dense at every batch width, so
keeping expansions device-resident was an architectural requirement rather than an optimisation (b). Task 015
then measured what the per-column operators of task 014 actually delivered on a CPU: at most 1.75$\times$
(factored, $\varphi$-only, $P=20$) and below 1$\times$ for Lamb--Helmholtz almost everywhere (c). The
projection in (a) was only realized later, by the whole-slab dense and fused-kernel GPU paths of tasks 019--024
--- not by the per-column operators.
\textbf{Scope:} every panel here is an \emph{isolated} operator or stage microbenchmark --- no panel in this
figure runs an \texttt{fmm!} call, and none includes nearfield direct interactions. Contrast Figs.\ 3, 5 and
6, whose timings are complete end-to-end evaluations.
\textbf{Caveats:} in (a) the large-batch recurrence rows are projections --- only
$\min(\text{batch},\,\text{cap})$ expansions were timed and scaled
(\texttt{scaled\_from\_per\_expansion=true}). Panel (c) is a single-laptop measurement on a different machine,
scope, and normalization from every other figure here, so it is shown as a ratio only and its microseconds must
never be compared against the stage seconds of Fig.\ 3 or the step times of Figs.\ 5--6. Panel (b)'s
\texttt{per\_block\_launch} variant is omitted (catastrophic at small batch); Float32 was measured and is not the
lever --- 1.2--1.27$\times$ at large batch and slower below.}
