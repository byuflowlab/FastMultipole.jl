\fmnote{\textbf{Machine / regime:} \texttt{m12-1-7}, AMD EPYC 7763, Julia 1.11.7, OpenBLAS pinned at
process start to 1 and 64 threads; Float64; batch $=64$ expansions.
\textbf{Data:} task 019b, \texttt{data/smallp\_fallback\_layout/m12-1-7/crossover\_\{isolated,stage\}\_blas\{1,64\}.csv}
(tables \texttt{fig01a\_*}, \texttt{fig01b\_*}, \texttt{fig01c\_*}).
\textbf{Reads as:} the isolated per-column 014 operators are slower than the production recurrence at
every $P\le6$ and in every BLAS regime; the materialized path only reaches parity near $P=12$ and the
factored path crosses break-even near $P=8$ for $\varphi$-only, while for Lamb--Helmholtz neither
variant ever wins in this range. In contrast, the whole-slab concat path already wins at small $P$,
then approaches or crosses back through parity as order and the $\chi$ channel increase.
\textbf{Caveats:} rows 1 and 3 are the isolated operator harness at batch 64; row 2 is the
\texttt{tiny\_parent} stage harness (3096 routes). They share a machine and per-expansion/per-route
normalization but not harness scope, so they are deliberately adjacent rather than overlaid.
The isolated kernels are scalar per-column, so per-expansion time is essentially
batch-independent --- the batch choice does not change the conclusion, and the 64-thread BLAS curves
lie on top of the single-thread ones. Fig.\ 2 expands the whole-slab comparison across route counts
and adds both the 019b corner and integrated 024 GPU-vs-host results.}
