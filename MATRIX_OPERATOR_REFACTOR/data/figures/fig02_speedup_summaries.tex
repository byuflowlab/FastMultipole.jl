\fmnote{\textbf{Machines / regimes:} (a--c) \texttt{m12-1-7}, AMD EPYC 7763, OpenBLAS pinned at process
start to 1 and 64 threads, Float64. (d) \texttt{m13h-1-1}, NVIDIA H200 (140\,GiB, CUDA 12.8),
device-resident lifecycle, \texttt{ConcatenatedFixedZM2L}. (e) matched task-024 uniform Float64
$P=4$ cases: \texttt{m12-2-17} EPYC 7763 BLAS=1 vs \texttt{m13h-1-1} H200, fastest eligible
resident strategy on each platform.
\textbf{Data:} task 019b, \texttt{crossover\_stage\_blas\{1,64\}.csv} and
\texttt{m13h-1-1/gpu\_smallp.csv}; task 024,
\texttt{operator\_ab\_benchmark/summary/case\_rankings.csv}
(tables \texttt{fig02a\_*}, \texttt{fig02b\_*}, \texttt{fig02c\_*}, \texttt{fig02d\_*}).
\textbf{Reads as:} unlike the per-column operators of Fig.\ 1, the whole-slab concat path wins on the CPU
at small $P$ ($7.6\times$ at $P=1$, $1.7\times$ at $P=4$) but gives the advantage back for
Lamb--Helmholtz at $P\ge6$. The explicit route-count panel answers the ``vs routes'' question directly and
its answer is no: route count alone does not predict the win. The largest speedup in the whole sweep
($7.6\times$) is at the \emph{smallest} population, 3096 routes, while 11.4M routes gives $1.9\times$ and
8 routes --- a legitimate tiny-batch extreme, not an outlier --- gives $3.2\times$. Order and channel
dominate; ``batch'' is not one number. The 019b H200 corner reaches
$\sim\!860\times$ end-to-end at $n=10^4$ against its single-thread host mirror; the integrated 024
comparison is deliberately more conservative and compares the fastest eligible resident strategy
on each platform at matched uniform Float64 cases.
\textbf{Scope:} panels (a--c) time the far-field M2L stage alone (per route); panel (d)'s lifecycle
series and panel (e)'s recurring step are complete evaluations including nearfield direct pairs,
which are evaluated inside L2B. These scopes must not be multiplied together.
\textbf{Caveats:} panel (c) is a scatter, not a curve --- the route count is non-monotonic in $P$
(\texttt{small\_constp} runs 8, 59334, 144290, 31254, 144290, 15492 for $P=1,2,3,4,6,8$), so the points
carry no left-to-right ordering in $P$; where two orders report the same route count the better speedup
is drawn. It shows BLAS=1 only; the $P=4$ BLAS=64 route slice is in
\texttt{fig02c\_concat\_speedup\_routes.csv} and its $P$-axis view is panel (b).
The three CPU configurations differ in $n$ and tree depth
(tiny $n{=}256,\ell{=}2$; small $n{=}2048,\ell{=}3$; medium $n{=}20000,\ell{=}4$) and only $P\in\{2,4\}$ was
run for medium --- gaps are genuine missing points, not zeros. Panel (d) points also differ in $\ell$
($n{=}128{:}\,\ell{=}2$; $512{:}\,3$; $1000{:}\,3$; $10^4{:}\,4$), and its host baseline is the
single-thread host mirror of the same path, not the tuned legacy CPU FMM. The M2L-vs-host-recurrence
point at $n=10^4$ was not recorded. Panel (e) compares different machines
(\texttt{m12-2-17} EPYC BLAS=1 and \texttt{m13h-1-1} H200), but identical 024
case definitions and complete recurring-step timing; it is a platform comparison, not a kernel-only ratio.}
